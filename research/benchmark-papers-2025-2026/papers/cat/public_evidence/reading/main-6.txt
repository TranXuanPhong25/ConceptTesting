4.2.2. Impact of Call-Chain Extraction and Dependency Resolution.
To investigate the contributions of call-chain awareness and dependency resolution in CAT, we compare CAT with three variants: CATvanilla, which excludes both types of contextual information extracted in Section 3.2; CATchain, which retains only call-chain context; and CATdep, which retains only dependency context. As shown in Table 6, CAT achieves 82.01% line coverage, 73.23% branch coverage, and a 67.80% mutation score. In comparison, even the best-performing variant, CATdep, achieves only 66.30% line coverage, 59.42% branch coverage, and a 53.04% mutation score. These results show that both types of contextual information contribute meaningfully and complementarily to test generation; retaining either alone substantially degrades performance. This complementarity stems from the different information they provide to the LLM. Call-chain context identifies feasible invocation paths to a focal method, whereas dependency context provides the information needed to construct and initialize the objects involved in those paths. For example, knowing an entry method in a call chain may be insufficient if the LLM lacks information about how to instantiate its declaring class or satisfy its dependencies. Conversely, dependency information alone may enable the construction of relevant objects but does not reveal how they can be composed into an invocation path that reaches the focal method. Combining the two therefore enables the LLM to both identify suitable invocation paths and construct the program states required to exercise them.

Precision of the extracted call chains. Because CAT relies on CHA (Section 3.2.1), some call chains passed to the LLM may be infeasible. We therefore examined all call paths that the static analysis passed into the prompts and identified those in which a virtual call resolves to an implementation incompatible with the receiver passed from the caller. Such spurious paths account for 2.41% of all paths. A representative example occurs in Codec: Soundex.difference and RefinedSoundex.difference pass this to the package-private helper SoundexUtils.difference, which then calls encode on it. CHA resolves this call to every implementation of StringEncoder, including DaitchMokotoffSoundex.encode, and therefore presents Soundex.difference as an indirect entry point for the focal method DaitchMokotoffSoundex.encode, although no execution of Soundex.difference reaches it. Such paths add noise to the call-chain and dependency contexts, but they are rare, and a test built on a spurious entry point simply does not cover the focal method.
Indirect invocation. The call-chain context allows CAT to reach a focal method through other methods on its call chains, which is useful when the focal method is hard to invoke directly. Table 7 quantifies the contribution of such indirect tests. We classify a retained test as indirect if it does not call the focal method in its body but executes it through a call chain, and we measure the gain attributable to indirect tests as the difference between the coverage and mutation score of the complete test suite and those of the same suite without the indirect tests. Across all projects, 25.9% of retained CAT tests are indirect on average, and they contribute 18.5, 15.6, and 13.5 pp to line coverage, branch coverage, and mutation score, respectively. The share of indirect tests differs considerably between projects. In Csv, 34.0% of the retained tests are indirect, and without them, line coverage, branch coverage, and mutation score would be lower by 17.1, 17.3, and 18.1 pp, respectively. In Collections, by contrast, only 1.6% of the tests are indirect; they add 2.6 pp to line coverage but hardly affect branch coverage (0.5 pp) and mutation score (0.0 pp), likely because most of its focal methods belong to utility classes with public static methods, such as IterableUtils, IteratorUtils, and MapUtils, for which valid direct invocations can be easily constructed.

RQ2 Summary
Call-chain and dependency contexts are complementary. On Defects4J, CAT improves line coverage, branch coverage, and mutation score over CATvanilla by 18.02, 15.88, and 18.42 pp (28.16%, 27.69%, and 37.30%), whereas using either context alone yields improvements of at most 2.31, 2.07, and 3.66 pp (CATdep). 4.2.3. CAT Performance on Unseen Projects.
As described in Section 4.1.1, we evaluate CAT on four projects that did not appear in the training data of the employed LLM and are more challenging, and compare its performance with PANTA and PANTAtb. Table 8 reports the line coverage, branch coverage, and mutation score results on these unseen projects. Across all projects, CAT consistently outperforms PANTA and PANTAtb, improving the average line and branch coverage by 13.58 and 12.02 pp (25.09% and 25.91%), respectively, over PANTA, and by 7.80 and 7.48 pp (13.02% and 14.68%) over PANTAtb. CAT also achieves the highest mutation score on every unseen project, 50.75% on average, compared with 30.87% for PANTAtb and 26.11% for PANTA, i.e., 19.88 and 24.64 pp (64.40% and 94.37%) higher. Compared with EvoSuite, CAT improves the average line coverage, branch coverage, and mutation score by 23.19, 14.16, and 17.30 pp (52.10%, 31.99%, and 51.72%) and achieves higher line coverage and mutation score on all four projects. On Binance, however, EvoSuite achieves much higher branch coverage than CAT (88.19% vs. 67.70%) but a lower mutation score (62.21% vs. 65.97%); as on Defects4J, higher coverage does not necessarily yield a higher mutation score.

In contrast to Defects4J subjects, which are predominantly single-module repositories where classes are organized within a single module, the new projects are multi-module systems involving multiple source directories and more complex inter-module dependencies (as discussed in Section 4.1.1). As a result, successful test generation requires reasoning about more intricate dependency structures and execution contexts across modules, where the advantages of call-chain-guided dependency resolution in CAT become more pronounced.
Compared with RQ1, CAT achieves 82.01% line coverage and 73.23% branch coverage on Defects4J, while reaching 67.70% and 58.42% on unseen projects. Such projects often involve deeper call chains, richer third-party dependencies, and more intricate object initialization patterns, all of which make it more challenging to construct valid execution contexts.
Nevertheless, CAT consistently outperforms both LLM-based baselines across all unseen projects, confirming that the proposed call-chain- and dependency-aware context modeling strategy generalizes effectively beyond benchmark-specific settings. Importantly, because these projects postdate the employed LLM’s cut-off, the observed gains cannot be explained by memorization and instead indicate genuine improvements in context construction and test generation.

Similar to RQ1, CAT demonstrates statistically significant improvements over both PANTAtb and PANTA on unseen projects for both line and branch coverage (p<0.01p<0.01). RQ3 Summary
On the four unseen projects (released after the employed LLM), CAT outperforms PANTA and PANTAtb on every project and in every metric. On average, it improves line coverage by 13.58 and 7.80 pp (25.09% and 13.02%), branch coverage by 12.02 and 7.48 pp (25.91% and 14.68%), and mutation score by 24.64 and 19.88 pp (94.37% and 64.40%), respectively, at about three times the generation time of PANTA. Compared with EvoSuite, CAT improves average line coverage, branch coverage, and mutation score by 23.19, 14.16, and 17.30 pp (52.10%, 31.99%, and 51.72%), although EvoSuite achieves higher branch coverage on Binance. Because these projects are unlikely to be part of the LLM’s training data, the gains are unlikely to be explained by memorization and can instead be attributed to CAT’s call-chain- and dependency-aware context modeling. Although absolute coverage decreases on these more complex projects, CAT maintains clear advantages over all baselines, demonstrating its robustness in realistic project-level test-generation scenarios.
4.3. Threats to Validity
Internal validity.
The performance of LLM-based approaches can be influenced by multiple factors, including the choice of LLM, prompt design, and configuration settings. To mitigate this threat, we adopt a state-of-the-art open-source LLM and use the same prompt design and configuration settings as PANTA to ensure a fair comparison.
In addition, while PANTA generates tests for all focal methods within a class in a single run, our approach CAT generates tests for each focal method individually. As discussed at the beginning of Section 4, to ensure a fair comparison under this methodological difference, we also include a time-bounded variant of PANTA, denoted PANTAtb, executed under the same time budget as CAT.
Another threat lies in the chosen parameter settings, such as Ng​e​nN_{gen} and dm​a​xd_{max}, which may affect both the effectiveness and efficiency of CAT. Although these values are determined through preliminary experiments, alternative configurations may lead to different performance trade-offs. However, because PANTAtb is evaluated under the same time budget, this threat is unlikely to invalidate the comparisons against the baseline, even if the absolute coverage numbers may vary. Moreover, LLM outputs are non-deterministic, and because of the cost of the evaluation, each LLM-based approach was run once per focal class, so the results for some focal classes may differ between runs. However, our conclusions do not rest on individual classes: we analyze the results aggregated over all 242 focal classes of the 18 projects, which reduces the influence of run-to-run variation in individual classes on the overall comparison. In addition, CAT reduces its dependence on single LLM responses by generating tests over up to Ng​e​nN_{gen} iterations and retaining only tests that compile and pass; all LLM-based approaches use the same low temperature (0.2), and the results are aggregated over 242 focal classes.
Another threat concerns the precision of the static analysis. CHA over-approximates virtual dispatch and may thus introduce spurious call chains, which account for 2.41% of the paths passed into the prompts (Section 4.2.2). Conversely, like other static call-graph analyses, it may miss calls made through reflection, dynamic proxies, or callbacks registered at run time; in addition, paths longer than dm​a​xd_{max} are excluded to avoid path explosion, and entry points that are not publicly accessible are filtered out, although a test class in the package of the focal class could also call package-private and protected methods of this package (Section 3.2.1). Since only 25.9% of the retained tests of CAT reach their focal method through a call chain on average, this restriction affects only part of the generated tests; admitting such entry points and invoking package-private and protected focal methods directly may nevertheless improve coverage of some focal methods.
